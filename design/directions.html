Names, dates and sources are from the research log; the review stamps are illustrative. Scroll inside each phone; switch themes above.

Phone mock · scrolls

Tone. Quiet, documentary, trustworthy. It looks like the registers it is built from, so the evidence ladder feels native rather than bolted on.

Type. Newsreader for names and headings, Source Serif 4 for text, IBM Plex Mono for signatures, IDs and stamps. All three carry Czech diacritics properly; Newsreader's italics do the Czech name forms (Taubrová, Konečná) justice.

Lineages. A register: one ruled entry per generation, Roman generation numbers in the margin, sign./folio references and open questions as marginalia. Branches collapse to "+ 3 siblings".

Fits best if the site's main readers are the serious ones (you, Karel's reviewers, the citizenship file). Risk: the migration map and timeline (v1) have to be drawn in the same ink-on-paper idiom or they will look pasted in.

Tone. Kinetic and legible. The story the family actually tells each other is the crossing, and this direction puts it on every page.

Type. Bricolage Grotesque for headlines (strong háčky, a little personality), Source Sans 3 for text, Barlow Condensed caps for place and station labels the way maps letter them.

Lineages. A route: a vertical line with one station per generation, the place as the station name, the ocean as a dashed gap. The Wood line joins as a second line at the 1947 station; the Tauber/Král line as a branch into Manitowoc.

Fits best if the v1 map and timeline are the heart of the site, because here they are not add-ons; the spine already is a map. Risk: the pre-1800 colonial generations have few places and the route can feel thin there.

Tone. Warm, bold, celebratory; made for the cousins who will read it on a phone at a reunion, not only for the researchers.

Type. Syne 800 for names and headlines (the háček becomes a signature mark), Manrope for text, JetBrains Mono for dates and IDs.

Lineages. Threads: a red Konečný ribbon and an indigo Wood ribbon run down the page and tie at the 1947 wedding; big generation numerals; the ribbon dashes where a person is living. Tauber/Král and Toepel get their own colours.

Fits best if you want the site to feel like a book the family is proud of, with scroll-driven chapters later. Risk: bold colour has to be rationed on dense evidence pages, or the ladder shouts.
